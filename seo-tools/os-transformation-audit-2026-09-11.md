# OS-transformation audit — 2026-09-11

Scope: the "SEO Operating System" brief (12-site version) applied to the portfolio as it actually is today.
Everything below is measured or read from the repository; where something is not known it says so.
Measurements come from `seo-tools/daily/os_measure.py` (output `os_audit_measurements.json`, same folder).

**Headline corrections to the brief before anything else**

- The portfolio has **11 sites**, not 12. No twelfth domain exists in `_sites.py`, GSC, or the ledger.
- Every `[FILL: …]` in the brief is still empty. The brief forbids inventing them, so §0 (team, commercial model,
  compliance regime) is **not filled** here; the fields that need the owner are listed at the end.
- The brief describes "a dashboard that is a hand-edited document". That is not what exists. The current
  dashboard is generated by scripts from API pulls; the fraction of hand-typed numbers is small and is itemised in §3.

Deployment: static HTML pushed to the `dashboard` branch of `alghostrader/jamal`, served by Vercel
(`vercel.json`: no build, `cleanUrls`). No Vercel CLI, no build step, no server. Payload today: 33 HTML files,
largest `performance.html` 267 KB, `today.html` 265 KB, `links.html` 163 KB, `rankings.html` 143 KB, `index.html`
65 KB. The "deploy payload limit" that forced the brief's thin-shell design does not apply to a git push; the
limit here is only readability of the branch diff.

Auth: Firebase email/password gate on every page (project `iptv-sales`), one sign-in per browser.
State: Firestore `seo_state/{uid}` (task states, link ticks, placement URLs) written by the browser, read by the
audit through a service account with Cloud Datastore Viewer.

## 7. Duplication and footprint — measured on 546 live pages

**Hosting.** All 11 sites resolve to Vercel anycast ranges (216.150.1.x / 216.150.16.x; smarters-live on
216.198.79.1). Shared provider, not a private network; this is the weakest footprint signal and is normal for
Vercel-hosted sites.

**Analytics IDs.** Four distinct GA4 IDs on four sites, none shared. No shared ID across sites.

**Templates (homepage class-name overlap).** iptvned / iptvpix 0.95 (same template), iptvfranceofficiel /
abonnementiptvofficiel 0.93 (same template), pix / shqip 0.69, ned / shqip 0.68. Everything else under 0.35.

**Near-duplicate copy (6-word shingle Jaccard, body text, nav/footer removed).** Only one site pair carries real
duplicates: iptvfranceofficiel.fr and abonnementiptvofficiel.com share 5 page pairs above 0.5, the refund policy
at 0.92. The "à propos" pages of spf / ifo / aio overlap at 0.37–0.39. No "what is IPTV" block is repeated across
nine sites; the article corpus is distinct.

**Reciprocal links between owned sites.** Three reciprocal pairs, all inside the French cluster: pix ↔ spf,
aio ↔ ifo, aio ↔ spf. One-way: smarters-live → prime (11 links), prime → ifo (4), pix → prime (2), pix → ifo (1).
No cross-links out of the ES, NL, AL or PL sites.

**Registrant.** Not checked (no WHOIS pull in this audit).

Risk register, by weight: (1) the FR reciprocal cluster with two identical templates and a cloned refund policy is
the pattern to unwind; (2) ned/pix identical template across two markets is low risk but visible; (3) hosting and
analytics are not a footprint.

## 8. Cannibalisation — measured from Search Console, same market, both sites in the top 50

29 queries. The two that matter:

- **ES: iptvsegura is ranking on iptvesp's Telegram cluster**, against the lane rule. "listas iptv telegram"
  segura #4 vs esp #4.1 (217 impressions), "iptv telegram españa" segura #2 vs esp #1.7, "grupo iptv telegram"
  segura #1 vs esp #4.6, "m3u iptv telegram" segura #3 vs esp #5.3. All from
  `/guias/iptv-telegram-riesgos` and the segura homepage. Segura's impressions on these are 1–3 each, esp's are
  in the hundreds, so esp owns them; segura's page should point at esp's article and drop the list intent.
- **FR: abonnementiptvofficiel's /boitier-iptv page ranks for prime's head terms**: "abonnement iptv 12 mois"
  aio #5 vs prime #30 (98 impressions on prime), "iptv abonnement 12 mois" aio #3 vs prime #44, "iptv prix",
  "iptv france", "fournisseur iptv" (prime #2 and aio #2 tied). The boîtier page outranks prime's own money pages
  on prime's own lane. This is the biggest strategic decision in the portfolio: either prime is the FR head-term
  site and aio links those terms to it, or aio is.

The remaining conflicts are low-impression overlaps between spf, prime and aio on "iptv abonnement …" variants.

## 10. Migration plan — what the brief asks for versus what to actually do

The brief's architecture (Postgres + RLS + a remote bundle stored in the database) solves a problem this
portfolio does not have (a deploy payload limit) at the cost of a new hosted database, new credentials and a
rewrite of a generator that already stores measured facts, decides, records and re-measures. The honest
recommendation is to **keep the generator and add the brief's missing capabilities to it**, in this order:

1. **Cannibalisation and footprint on the dashboard** — done today (Rankings and Authority pages), re-measured every audit.
2. **Owner decisions the data now demands** — ES Telegram cluster ownership, FR head-term ownership (prime vs aio),
   unwind the FR reciprocal links and the cloned refund policy.
3. **Vercel token** → real deploy states and BLOCKED cards (already coded, waiting on the credential).
4. **Sales read path** — agree the Firestore collection shape with the sales session, then show revenue per site
   with "owner-entered" as its source label.
5. **GA4 on all 11 sites, one property per site** — without it, sessions and conversion stay "Not connected".
6. **Outcome engine at 56 days and the noise threshold** (5 positions) — the 7/14/28-day checkpoints exist; add 56 and the threshold.
7. **Semrush position-tracking or a per-keyword "all owned URLs" probe** — only if the cannibalisation view from GSC proves insufficient.
8. **A database** — only when two people need to write state concurrently from outside the browser. Firestore
   already covers the browser; git covers the audit.

Phases 1–2 of the brief (schema, RLS, thin shell) are therefore **not started**, deliberately, pending the owner's
answer to the architecture question and to the `[FILL]` fields.

## 11. Fields the brief requires from the owner

- Sites: for each of the 11, what it sells, platform, money/feeder/landing role (partly known from lanes).
- Team: who can change hosting, DNS, CMS, analytics, payments.
- Commercial model: subscription vs one-off vs reseller, AOV, shared billing backend.
- Compliance regime: licensed content and territories, forbidden claims, required disclaimer text and placement.
  Current standing rules cover part of this (no rights-holder / channel / league names, no "100% legal", brand
  anchors only, European markets only).
- Architecture decision: extend the current generator (recommended) or build the Postgres/remote-bundle system.
